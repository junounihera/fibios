# Secure Data Environments and the Access Turn

For two decades the default way to give a researcher NHS data was to give them a copy. That model produced thousands of separate extract agreements, each negotiated from scratch. The NHS Research Secure Data Environment Network is the attempt to end it. Data stays inside an NHS-controlled environment and the researcher goes in, rather than the data going out. Twelve environments now exist. A separate company is being built to run the door.

## Twelve environments, one network

The Network is made up of twelve secure data environments: one England-wide environment run by NHS England, and eleven regional ones. NHS England funds all twelve through its Data for Research and Development programme, and the regional environments cover areas such as the West Midlands, the South West, the North West, the North East and North Cumbria, and Yorkshire and the Humber.

The design intent is consolidation. An account of the programme published by Cancer Research UK put the number of separate data access points in England at around 7,000 and described the aim as reducing them to twelve. Regional environments hold more detailed local datasets; the England environment holds national datasets.

One point of confusion is worth naming. One regional explainer states there are ten regional secure data environments plus a national one led by NHS England. The West Midlands environment and Understanding Patient Data both state twelve in total, one national and eleven regional. The twelve figure is the one repeated across the network's own members, and the discrepancy sits in a single regional page.

## What the access turn actually changes

A secure data environment inverts the direction of travel. Under the old model a researcher received a dataset, held it, and the NHS lost visibility of what happened to it afterwards. Under the new model the researcher submits code that runs against data held inside the environment, and receives outputs rather than records.

That change is what makes the governance claims possible. The environment can log every query, restrict what can leave, and revoke access without chasing a copy that has already been downloaded. It also moves the technical difficulty from extraction to review: someone has to decide whether a proposed output could re-identify a patient before it is released.

The Health Research Authority has been closely involved in the network's governance, and in October 2026 it recorded a milestone: all regional secure data environments in England are now established and continuing to mature. The Authority's confidentiality advice service sits alongside the environment approvals, advising on the use of confidential patient information.

## Behind the single front door

The Health Data Research Service is the part most often described as a single front door, and the description hides more than it explains. HDRS is not itself a store of data. It is being built as a service layer over environments that already exist, including the SDE Network, and its stated functions are a single point of access, standardised commercial agreements, standardised data access approvals, and accredited research environments.

The funding was announced by the Prime Minister on 7 April 2025: up to £600 million, of which up to £500 million from government and £100 million pledged by Wellcome. The service is to be housed at the Wellcome Genome Campus in Cambridgeshire. Baroness Nicola Blackwood was appointed chair on 25 November 2025.

HDRS is a government-owned company, not a division of the Department of Health and Social Care. The department's own recruitment pack for the chair describes "a new Government-owned company set up to deliver a world-class service", directly accountable to ministers through the department's permanent secretary, with a framework agreement to follow. The sponsor department has reserved matters as shareholder; the board is the decision-making body for everything else. That structure is a choice, and it means the service will have its own accounts and its own performance record rather than being reported inside the department's.

What the front door is made of, in other words, is contracts, approvals and accredited environments, sitting over data that stays where it is. The datasets are the twelve environments and other national sources; HDRS is the layer that decides who gets in and on what terms.

## What is scheduled, and what is not

The published schedule is a sequence rather than a launch date. The service's own roadmap describes 2026 as a year of decisions and design covering governance, infrastructure and how researchers will work with health data. Wellcome states the aim is to launch the first elements of the service by the end of 2026, with further capabilities rolling out progressively. The roadmap places capability growth, wider data availability and infrastructure maturity across 2027 to 2029, with the ambition of global leadership from 2030 onward.

Those are the announced milestones, and they are not the same as delivered capability. Three things are genuinely undecided in public. First, which datasets are available on the first day, as opposed to which are planned; the roadmap names expansion but not a first tranche. Second, how the single access point will join data held in more than one regional environment, which no retrieved document describes in operational terms. Third, whether the service will publish its own access statistics, and against what baseline, since the 7,000 access points figure describes a starting position that has never been audited in public.

The access turn is real and the twelve environments exist. Whether twelve is the right number, and whether a government-owned company is the right vehicle to sit above them, are questions the first year of published HDRS performance will answer better than any of the documents announcing it.

## Summary

The NHS has moved from handing researchers copies of data to holding data inside twelve secure environments and letting researchers in, and a new government-owned company is being built to run the access layer above them.

- **The network is twelve environments.** One England-wide environment run by NHS England, plus eleven regional ones, all funded through the Data for R&D programme.
- **Access replaces copies.** Researchers submit code that runs inside the environment and receive outputs, which is what makes query logging and revocation possible.
- **HDRS is a service layer, not a data store.** Its functions are a single access point, standardised agreements, standardised approvals and accredited environments.
- **It is a government-owned company.** The department's chair recruitment pack describes a company accountable to ministers, not a departmental division.
- **The schedule is a sequence.** Design through 2026, capability growth across 2027 to 2029, with first elements aimed for the end of 2026.

Three things remain unpublished: which datasets will be available on day one, how the single access point will join data held in more than one regional environment, and what access statistics the service will report against. The network's own milestone, that all regional environments are established and maturing, describes the infrastructure. It does not yet describe what researchers can do with it.
